02 — Granger Causality

Oil does not significantly
predict Dubai prices.

An F-test on each two-variable VAR tests whether past Brent values help forecast Dubai prices beyond the price series itself. The null: Brent does not Granger-cause Dubai prices. Nothing clears 5%. Villas reach p = 0.084 at a 3-month lag — borderline at 10%, directionally sensible.

04 — What This Tells Us

Dubai property does not
run on oil money.

The results challenge a common assumption. Over this 12-year window, Brent crude price swings did not significantly predict where Dubai residential prices moved.

01
No significant Granger causality in any segment. All p-values sit above the 5% threshold. Dubai property prices are not significantly predictable from past oil prices in any of the three residential segments over this study window.
02
Dubai's GDP does not depend on oil. The city's economy runs on trade, logistics, tourism, and finance. That is Abu Dhabi. Brent price swings have no direct channel into Dubai's fiscal position or public employment in the way they would in an oil-budget city.
03
The 2022–2024 boom was not an oil story. The largest price run in DLD history came from post-Ukraine migration, Golden Visa expansion, and an influx of buyers from Russia, Europe, and South Asia. None of that correlates with Brent crude.
04
Villas are the most interesting result. p = 0.084 at a 3-month lag is not significant by conventional standards, but it is not noise. If oil has any path into Dubai property, it is through GCC nationals — whose wealth tracks oil — buying in the luxury villa segment. FEVD caps that effect at 2.1%.
05
The 2022–2024 structural break reduces ADF power. The Dubai price surge was so sharp and non-oil-driven that ADF tests become inconclusive on first differences. KPSS tests confirm log-differences are stationary. Both tests together justify the transformation used in the VAR.
